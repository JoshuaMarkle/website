Every pound of concrete has to be paddled around the course, but the hull still has to survive four people kneeling in it. JAGUAR finds the lightest canoe that still passes every structural check.</p>

You give it a hull, the crews, and the team's concrete lab results. It searches wall thickness, mix density and mold depth, checks every candidate, and returns the lightest design that passes, along with which check is the limiting one.

## The checks

Each candidate is analyzed as a beam floating on its own displacement. JAGUAR solves for the waterline and trim that balance the hull and crew, then works out shear and bending along the length. Every check compares demand to capacity (D/C), and anything over 1.0 fails:

- **Strength comes from the team's lab breaks.** JAGUAR doesn't assume a concrete strength. It fits tensile strength against density from the team's cylinder tests, then warns when the winning mix is lighter than anything actually tested.
- **Freeboard and stability** are hard limits: the crew must float with 6 inches to spare, and the loaded canoe has to keep a minimum metacentric height (GM) with the paddlers anywhere within 3 inches of their seats.
- **Results are re-checked.** The winner is re-run at twice the hull resolution, and D/C is extrapolated across 161, 321 and 641 stations. The output only says `VERIFIED` if the answer holds.

Exit codes are meaningful (0 for a verified design, 2 when nothing passes, with the reason), so the tool fits into scripts. When the winner sits at the edge of a search range, it says to widen the range.

## Searching the shape

Given a fixed hull, the wall is the only variable. With a shape search enabled, JAGUAR also designs the hull. An outer loop proposes shapes (length, beam, draft, waterplane fullness, rocker, section shape) and generates offsets for each. An inner loop finds the lightest wall for that hull in about a tenth of a second. The outer search starts with 96 Latin-hypercube samples, then switches to pattern search.

Left alone, a weight-only objective would shrink the canoe into a narrow log. The freeboard, strength and stability gates are what keep the answer a boat.

## Racing it

Weight isn't the actual goal. Race time is. The race simulator plans a path around the ASCE buoy courses, with a free turn radius at each mark and tangent lines between them. It integrates the boat's speed along that path using paddler power and thrust limits, wave resistance (Michell thin-ship theory), ITTC friction, and a cross-flow model for drag in turns. It also reports how sensitive each event is to straight-line drag versus turning drag, which sets how the shape search weighs the two.

With `objective = "race"`, the shape search minimizes a weighted time across every event at regionals or nationals. That pick ends up longer and heavier: 21 ft 4 in and 99.8 lb. Its weighted race time is 99.0 s, against 104.1 s for the lightest shape.

## Getting a design out

Hulls come in from Michlet offset files or the built-in generator. The winner goes out as a JSON report, or as a STEP or STL solid for the mold shop. `jaguar serve` opens a browser view with the canoe in 3D, editable inputs, and live charts as candidates come in: D/C along the hull, freeboard, sections and mix zones.
