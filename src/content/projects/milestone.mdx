## What it does

- **Three ways to log a drive.** Start a live timer when you pull out and stop it when you park. Enter a past drive by hand. Or save a template for a drive you repeat, like the commute to school, and log it in one tap.
- **Day and night goals.** Each drive is tagged day or night, with weather and an optional note. The dashboard tracks total time and progress toward each goal. You set your own goals, since requirements differ by state.
- **History you can fix.** Every drive is a card you can open, edit or delete, since people forget to stop timers.
- **Export to PDF.** The whole log becomes a printable driving log sheet with per-drive rows and day and night totals, ready to hand in with a license application.
- **Study guides.** Built-in reading on the rules of the road and preparing for the written and road tests, rendered from Markdown bundled with the app.
- **First-run walkthrough.** A welcome screen sets your goals, and coach marks point out each part of the dashboard.

Everything is stored on the phone with Hive, a small key-value database for Flutter. There's no account, no server and nothing to sign up for.

## Building it

I picked Flutter because Dart felt close to the C# I already knew from Unity, and because one codebase could target both Android and iOS. The first prototype had no UI at all, just enough code to learn the language. Then I mocked screens up in InVision and went through several UI overhauls before the dashboard felt simple enough.

The hardest bug came late: a database malfunction that forced a large rewrite of the data layer, and later a fix to how logs are indexed. Android shipped through a Play Store testing track. iOS stalled because publishing requires Xcode, and I didn't have a Mac.
